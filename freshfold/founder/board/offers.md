# Offers lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Real, recurring pain.** Laundry comes back every week, so one won customer can mean repeat revenue, and the pitch frames the dream outcome ("get your time back") well.
- **Time delay is the best part of the offer.** Same-day turnaround (morning drop, evening pickup) is a concrete, easy-to-say claim on the value equation's time variable.
- **Clear pricing.** Charging by the kg with two published rates removes the bill confusion that item counting causes. That lowers friction at first purchase.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Effort is left on the buyer.** The customer still has to carry clothes to a shop and come back the same day. For "busy" buyers, two trips is the very sacrifice they are trying to escape. Effort is where this offer scores worst on the value equation, and pickup/delivery is not mentioned.
2. **Commodity on a price sheet.** Nothing here makes saying no feel stupid. Sorting, "good detergents" and folding are table stakes. Whether ₹50/₹60 per kg is cheap, at market or premium is unknown (/founder-competitors). If the main difference turns out to be price, it is a commodity.
3. **Four markets, no first customer.** Hostel/PG students, professionals, bachelors and families differ in pain, money and reachability. Students may have the pain but not the budget, and families may want a different service. A blurry market sinks even a good offer. Which segment buys is unknown (/founder-consumer).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one segment, in which neighbourhood, is first, and why does it have both the pain and the money?
- Why would a buyer believe same-day and "clothes you'll be happy to wear"? What proof is there: trial, reviews, a guarantee?
- What happens when a garment is damaged, lost or late? What do you promise?
- Could pickup and delivery, a weekly subscription or a minimum order be delivered profitably? That depends on the cost per kg and capacity, which are unknown (/founder-cfo).
- Could you charge more than the going rate and still be the obvious choice? What would have to be in the offer for that to work?
- Does same-day still hold at peak volume, and at what daily kg does it break?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one first segment and one neighbourhood, backed by panel evidence (/founder-consumer).
2. Cut buyer effort: test pickup/delivery or a pickup-point option, costed by /founder-cfo.
3. Add a guarantee the numbers can afford, for example on on-time delivery or garment damage, plus a weekly plan that gives a reason to commit. Build it with /founder-offer.
4. Map local competitor prices and positioning before fixing the price (/founder-competitors).
