# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Small markets are where the Monopoly lens wants you to start.** A single laundry shop in one Lucknow neighbourhood is small enough that one operator could become the default choice there. That beats chasing a city-wide market.
- **Same-day turnaround and simple per-kg pricing are clear promises.** If they hold at volume, they could make the shop the dependable option on its block. That is how a local brand begins.
- **People do laundry every week.** If the shop gets loyalty, repeat visits could build density and, over time, some economies of scale (machine use, bulk detergent).

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing stops a copycat.** Per-kg pricing, sorting, good detergent and same-day service are improvements on what already exists, not a breakthrough, and the founder names no secret. A dhobi, a chain or a pickup app could match all of it within months. If that happens, the business is left competing on price for thin margins.
2. **It has no plan for reaching customers.** It's a walk-in shop with no stated location, no pickup or delivery and no first customer group. Four segments are named (professionals, students, bachelors, families), so none of them is owned. We don't know how the first 100 customers arrive or what each one costs.
3. **The same-day promise may break at volume.** Daily capacity is unknown. If same-day fails when the shop gets busy, it loses the one thing that sets it apart, and nothing else protects it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who else washes, irons or picks up laundry within walking distance of your shop, and at what price per kg or per item? (/founder-competitors)
- Which one neighbourhood and which one customer group can you win first? A hostel/PG cluster? One office area? Which of these groups buys, and why? (/founder-consumer)
- What do you know about laundry in Lucknow that competitors don't?
- What is your cost per kg, and how many kg a day can you wash before same-day stops being possible? (/founder-cfo)
- How do the first 100 customers find you, and what does it cost to get each one?
- What would make customers stay if a competitor matched your price and speed in month six? Subscriptions, hostel contracts, pickup routes?
- Have you run a laundry or retail business before?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one tight cluster (for example, the PGs and hostels around one campus) and one customer group, and show you can reach most of it.
2. A competitor map shows a gap that nearby operators don't fill (/founder-competitors).
3. A capacity and cost model shows same-day still works at the target daily volume, with a margin left after price pressure (/founder-cfo).
4. A named way to keep customers from switching to a copycat, such as monthly plans, institutional contracts or route density, tested with real buyers first.
