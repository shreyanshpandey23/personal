# Board summary: FreshFold Laundry

## The vote
**3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass.** Average score **4.0 / 10** (Offers 4, Monopoly 3, Product 5).

The board likes the bones of the idea: weekly repeat need, simple per-kg pricing, a same-day promise. None of the three would back it as a walk-in shop serving everyone. All three would back a narrower version that removes the customer's trips and owns one neighbourhood.

## Risks more than one member raised (most serious first)
1. **"Get your time back" but the customer makes two trips.** All three members raised this. Walk-in drop-off and pick-up on the same day is the very chore busy customers want to escape. No pickup or delivery is mentioned.
2. **Four customer groups and no first one.** All three members raised this. Professionals, hostel/PG students, bachelors and families differ in budget, volume, schedule and where they live. A shop built for all four is built for none.
3. **Same-day may break at volume.** All three members raised this. Daily capacity and cost per kg are unknown. If same-day slips, or ironing gets rushed to hit it, the one thing that sets FreshFold apart disappears. → `/founder-cfo`, `/founder-ops`
4. **Easy to copy.** Offers and Monopoly both raised this. Sorting, good detergent and folding are what customers expect anyway. A dhobi, chain or pickup app could match per-kg pricing and same-day within months, and then it's a price fight. Where ₹50/₹60 per kg sits against local rates is unknown. → `/founder-competitors`
5. **No promise for when things go wrong.** Offers and Product both raised this. Nothing says what happens when a garment is lost, shrunk, stained or late.

## Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)
- [ ] One first customer group and one dense neighbourhood chosen, e.g. the PG/hostel cluster around one campus or one office area; the rest cut for now (`/founder-consumer`)
- [ ] A competitor map of dhobis, ironing stalls, laundry chains and pickup apps near that neighbourhood, with prices per kg or per item, shows a real gap (`/founder-competitors`)
- [ ] The customer's trips removed: the shop is on their daily route, or pickup/delivery is tested and costed (`/founder-offer`, `/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] Cost per kg (water, power, detergent, labour, ironing) and daily kg capacity show same-day holds at the target volume with a margin left (`/founder-cfo`, `/founder-ops`)
- [ ] A guarantee the numbers can afford, on same-day delivery and on damage or loss (`/founder-offer`)
- [ ] A reason to stay if a copycat matches the price: a weekly or monthly plan, hostel/PG contracts or pickup-route density (`/founder-offer`)
- [ ] The full customer journey written down: drop-off or pickup, tagging, the "ready" message, return, complaints (`/founder-ops`)
- [ ] Shop location, rent, machines, staff and setup budget known (`/founder-cfo`, `/founder-ops`)

## Where the board disagrees
- **How much the craft matters.** The Product lens rates sorting by colour and fabric, good detergent and proper drying as real quality that most customers never see. The Offers lens calls the same things table stakes that make nobody say yes. Both can be true: quality keeps customers, but it won't win the first one.
- **What could protect it.** The Monopoly lens wants an advantage that compounds, such as route density, contracts or subscriptions, and scores lowest because none exists yet. The Product and Offers lenses focus on making the first week's experience good enough to repeat.
- **Same-day as the hook.** Offers calls it the strongest part of the offer. Monopoly says it is copyable and protects nothing on its own.

## The strongest version the board can see
The strongest version is **narrower than the pitch**: *the weekly laundry plan for the PGs and hostels (or one office cluster) in one Lucknow neighbourhood, picked up and dropped back.* Choose the densest cluster of one customer group. Sell a weekly or monthly plan (for example, a fixed number of kg a month at a set price) rather than one-off walk-ins. Collect from the PG or office on a fixed route and return the same evening, so the customer makes no trips at all. Back it with a simple promise: back the same day or that load is free, and anything damaged is paid for. Route density in one neighbourhood keeps the cost of collection low and is hard for a single dhobi or a city-wide app to match. Keep the walk-in counter for whoever lives nearby. This differs from the pitch, which leads with a walk-in shop for four customer groups. The board thinks that version competes on price; this one competes on convenience and habit.
