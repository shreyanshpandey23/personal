# CFO's note: FreshFold Laundry

> **Almost every input is an estimate.** Only the prices (₹50/₹60 per kg) came from the founder. Rent, wages, machines, power and volume are the CFO's estimates; see `cfo-sources.md`. Get real quotes before relying on this. Not financial, tax or legal advice: have an accountant check the structure, payroll and GST before money moves.

**The margin.** Each kg sells for about **₹54** (a mix of ₹50 fold and ₹60 iron, less a small plan discount) and costs about **₹20** in detergent, power, water, bags, fuel and a damage reserve. That leaves **₹34 a kg**. Fixed costs are about **₹67,500 a month**: rent, 2 staff, 1 rider and running costs. So break-even is **67 kg a day**, about 2,000 kg a month, or roughly 125 subscribers washing 16 kg a month. At the plan of 80 kg a day, the margin is only **11%**.

**The line to watch: your machines can only do so much.** The shop can wash and dry about **100 kg a day** with 2 washers and 1 dryer, and still deliver same-day. Break-even at 67 needs the shop two-thirds full every day. That leaves little room:
- **20% less volume** makes the plan lose money (margin **-2%**).
- **A ₹25,000 salary for you** moves break-even to **91 kg a day**, right at the machines' limit.
- **Students going home** in summer cuts volume for about 2 months. That costs roughly ₹25,000 in year 1 even before anything else goes wrong.

**Cash.** In the base plan, **year 1 loses ₹2.86 lakh**. You'd need about **₹7.5 lakh** in total: ₹4.5 lakh to set up, plus ₹3 lakh of losses before monthly profit begins around month 11.

**Three ways to improve the margin (what-ifs run).**
1. **Charge ₹60 a kg across the board**, with ₹65 for ironing on one-off orders. At ₹60, break-even drops from 67 to **57 kg a day**, the plan margin rises from 11% to **20%** and year 1 improves by about ₹92,000. At ₹65, break-even is **50 kg a day**. Don't go *below* ₹54: a 10% cut takes the margin to 1%.
2. **Work in the shop yourself for year 1** instead of hiring a second staff member. Break-even falls to **55 kg a day** and year 1 improves by ₹1.44 lakh.
3. **Grow faster through PG-owner deals.** Signing PGs in bulk, rather than one student at a time, brings in volume sooner. With ₹60 pricing, yourself working, and a faster ramp reaching 90 kg a day by month 11, **year 1 makes ₹2.4 lakh** and the cash needed falls to **₹4.95 lakh**. At 90 kg a day, the shop earns about **₹52,500 a month** profit.

**Is it a business?** **Yes, but only if you do all three together.** With 1 and 2 alone, year 1 still loses ₹49,000. Speed of signing customers is what separates a loss from a profit. The setup spend is not earned back within year 1 in any scenario; with the combined plan it is earned back early in year 2.

**The board's money conditions.**
- [ ] Same-day holds at target volume with a margin left. **Only partly met.** Capacity is about 100 kg a day and break-even is 67 (or 47 with the fixes). Adding a second dryer raises capacity; check its cost.
- [ ] Pickup and drop is costed: ₹12,000 a month for a rider plus ₹2 a kg fuel. **Only affordable because the routes are clustered.**
- [ ] Rent, machines and setup budget: **estimates only.** Get real quotes.
- [ ] The monthly plan price is not set. The numbers say **₹55–60 a kg on a plan** (for example, 15 kg a month for ₹850–900) and **₹65 a kg for one-off orders**.
