# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The pain looks real on the hiring side.** Anyone who has to staff a wedding or a brand activation at short notice runs into no-shows and workers nobody has vetted. If ratings and past-event history actually predict who shows up, that raises the "likelihood" in the value equation, and likelihood is where most offers fail.
- **The time-to-result is short.** Organisers need people this week, not next quarter. An offer that fills a shift fast scores well on time delay.
- **Same-day pay is something workers would value.** For daily-wage workers it is a real stack item, and it makes money directly.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The market may be weak on "buyers have money" and "pain is massive".** Event staff in a single tier-2 city may be a low-budget, irregular, relationship-driven buy. If organisers already fill shifts cheaply through a WhatsApp group or a contractor they trust, the pain is mild and the platform is just a cheaper channel, which makes it a commodity. Whether that is true is unverified. Ask /founder-consumer and /founder-competitors.
2. **There is no offer yet, only a marketplace.** "Browse workers and hire" asks the buyer to do the work (effort and sacrifice go up). Nothing is guaranteed to the side that pays, and the 10-20% commission has no named payer. Without a promise like "staffed and shown up, or you don't pay", the company has little reason to choose this over its own contact list.
3. **"Guaranteed events" for subscribers is a promise the business may not be able to keep.** If demand is thin it becomes a broken guarantee that charges low-income workers for work that never comes, and that destroys trust on the supply side.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one buyer type has the sharpest pain and the most money: wedding organisers, brand promoters, college fests, or corporate events?
- How do they staff today, what does it cost, and what goes wrong (no-shows, late or unreliable workers)?
- What outcome would you guarantee the company, and could you afford it if workers fail to show?
- Why would an organiser believe your ratings over a contractor they already know?
- Who pays the commission, and could you charge more than a cheaper channel and still be the obvious choice?
- How exactly would "guaranteed events" be delivered, and what happens to a subscriber if it isn't?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Narrow to one buyer segment in Lucknow and confirm the pain and willingness to pay with real organisers (/founder-consumer), plus a map of how shifts are filled today (/founder-competitors).
2. Rebuild it as an offer for the buyer: a guaranteed outcome (for example, reliable workers who turn up or a replacement/refund), vetted workers, and fast fill. Use /founder-offer for this, and use /founder-cfo to confirm the guarantee and the commission are affordable.
3. Drop or redesign the worker "guaranteed events" subscription until demand is proven.
