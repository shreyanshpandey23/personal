# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The core moment is easy to picture: an organiser needs, say, a dozen staff for Saturday and fills the roster in minutes from people with a visible track record. If that moment really works, it is worth building toward.
- Showing past events and ratings speaks directly to the trust problem in casual hiring, which is "will this person show up and behave?".
- One launch city (Lucknow) keeps the first version small enough to be done well.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No single "one thing".** Four event types (marketing, leaflets, event management, weddings), two sides and three revenue streams make up four products, not one. Each event type has a different buyer, timeline and definition of a "good worker". A platform that tries to serve all of them at once will serve none of them brilliantly.
2. **The experience is decided off the platform, at the seams.** The thing that matters is a worker turning up on time, briefed and properly dressed, then getting paid without a fight. That happens at the venue, not in the app. When someone fails to show, the organiser blames Eventify. Who handles replacements, disputes and late payment, and how? Nobody has answered that yet.
3. **Revenue design that hurts the experience.** A "guaranteed events" subscription makes a promise the product may not be able to keep, because it depends on demand the business does not control. A same-day pay fee charges workers to fix a pain the platform should remove. It is also undecided who pays the 10–20% commission, and that choice shapes both sides' first five minutes. One more open point: the founder calls the workers "volunteers" although they are paid. That will confuse both sides.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is Eventify for, and who is it for first?
- Walk me through the first five minutes for a Lucknow wedding organiser who needs staff tomorrow. What do they see, tap and pay?
- What happens, step by step, when a hired worker does not show up?
- How are workers vetted before their first gig, when they have no ratings yet?
- How do organisers find staff today, and what exactly is painful about it? (This is unverified. /founder-consumer answers it, and /founder-competitors checks the "no organised platform" premise.)
- What would you cut from version one?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one event type and one buyer to start (for example, weddings or brand promotions, not both), and state the product in one sentence.
2. Write down the end-to-end experience for both sides, including no-shows, replacements and payment, before building.
3. Drop the "guaranteed events" subscription unless there is a concrete way to honour it. Make prompt payment the default, not a paid extra, unless /founder-cfo shows the economics require the fee.
4. Validate the pain and the current workaround with /founder-consumer and /founder-competitors before building beyond a manual pilot.
