# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **There is a small market to start in.** One city (Lucknow) and one segment, if the founder picks one, is the right size to dominate before expanding.
- **A two-sided marketplace can build network effects.** If the worker pool gets deep enough that organisers stop using alternatives, and gig flow gets steady enough that workers stop looking elsewhere, local liquidity is a real moat.
- **Ratings and event history could become proprietary data.** A verified record of who turned up and performed is hard for a newcomer to copy, but only once it exists at volume.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No secret, and a premise nobody has checked.** "There is no organised platform" is unverified. Gig-staffing and part-time job apps may already serve this buyer in Lucknow. If they do, this is an incremental improvement in a crowded market with thin margins. Open question for /founder-competitors.
2. **Disintermediation kills the take rate.** Organisers hire workers "directly" after seeing their profiles. Once they have met a good worker, nothing stops them paying that worker on WhatsApp next time and skipping the 10–20%. Event staffing is a repeat, local, relationship business, which makes this the textbook leak.
3. **No distribution plan, and a cold-start problem on both sides.** Nothing says how the first 100 organisers and the first few hundred workers arrive, or what each costs. The "guaranteed events" subscription promises supply-side liquidity the platform cannot yet deliver. Unknown CAC is an open question for /founder-cfo and /founder-consumer.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who else sells this to a Lucknow wedding planner or brand-activation manager today, and how do they find staff now (agencies, contractors, WhatsApp groups)?
- Which single segment will you own first, and what share of its event-staff bookings could you hold in 12 months?
- What do you know about this market that others do not?
- Why would an organiser book a worker through you a second time instead of calling them directly?
- How do the first 100 hiring customers arrive, and what does each cost?
- What stops a local staffing agency or an existing job app copying this in month six?
- How exactly is "guaranteed events" honoured when demand is thin?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A /founder-competitors map that confirms the gap, or names a segment nobody serves well.
2. One beachhead chosen (for example weddings or brand promotions in Lucknow), with an estimate of how many organisers it contains and how often they hire.
3. A named reason to stay on the platform (payment protection, no-show replacement, vetted attendance, compliance) that makes going around it cost more than the commission.
4. A distribution plan for the first 100 organisers, with an estimated cost per organiser from /founder-cfo.
5. Drop or redesign the "guaranteed events" subscription until there is enough demand to honour it.
