# Board summary: Eventify

## The vote
**3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass.** Average score **3.7 / 10** (Offers 4, Monopoly 3, Product 4).

No member would back it as pitched. All three would back a narrower version that starts with one event type, keeps the money on the hiring side, and gives organisers a reason to keep booking through the app.

## Risks more than one member raised (most serious first)
1. **The "guaranteed events" subscription.** All three members raised this. It charges low-income workers for work the platform cannot promise until organisers post jobs steadily. If that work doesn't come, it destroys trust on the worker side and looks like a job scam. Every member's conditions say to drop it or redesign it.
2. **Organisers will hire workers directly after the first booking.** Monopoly and Offers both raised this. Once an organiser has met a good worker, nothing stops them paying that worker on WhatsApp next time and skipping the 10–20%. Event staffing is local, repeat and built on relationships, which makes the leak very likely.
3. **No single focus.** Product and Offers both raised this. Weddings, marketing promotions, leaflet distribution and event management have different buyers, timelines and ideas of a "good worker". Add two sides and three revenue streams, and that is four products.
4. **"No organised platform" has not been checked.** Monopoly and Offers both raised this. Gig-staffing and part-time job apps may already serve Lucknow. How organisers fill shifts today (agencies, contractors, WhatsApp groups) is unknown. → `/founder-competitors`
5. **No plan for what happens when a worker doesn't show up.** Product and Offers both raised this. The product succeeds or fails at the venue. A no-show is blamed on Eventify, and nobody has said who replaces the worker or refunds the organiser.
6. **Both sides are empty on day one, and the first customers have no source.** Monopoly and Product both raised this. Nothing says how the first 100 organisers and first few hundred workers arrive, or what each costs. → `/founder-cfo`, `/founder-marketing`

## Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)
- [ ] One starting customer chosen, e.g. wedding organisers *or* brand promotions in Lucknow, not both, and the product stated in one sentence (`/founder-offer`)
- [ ] The pain and willingness to pay confirmed with real organisers, along with how they staff today and what goes wrong (`/founder-consumer`)
- [ ] A competitor map of gig-staffing apps, job apps, agencies and contractors serving Lucknow that confirms a real gap (`/founder-competitors`)
- [ ] It is decided who pays the 10–20% commission, preferably the organiser, and `/founder-cfo` confirms it covers costs
- [ ] A guarantee to the organiser, e.g. "your workers turn up, or we replace them or refund you", priced so the business can afford it (`/founder-offer`, `/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] A named reason for both sides to keep booking through Eventify (payment protection, no-show replacement, verified attendance, insurance or compliance) that costs more to give up than the commission (`/founder-offer`)
- [ ] The "guaranteed events" worker subscription dropped or redesigned until demand is proven
- [ ] The full experience written down for both sides, including vetting new workers who have no ratings, briefing, no-shows, disputes and payment (`/founder-ops`)
- [ ] A plan for the first 100 organisers and the first workers, with an estimated cost per organiser (`/founder-marketing`, `/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] A manual pilot (WhatsApp plus a spreadsheet) before any app is built (`/founder-launch`)
- [ ] "Volunteers" renamed to "event staff" or "crew" in the brand and pitch, because the workers are paid (`/founder-brand`)
- [ ] Budget, team and tech plan known (`/founder-cfo`, `/founder-ops`)

## Where the board disagrees
- **The same-day payment fee.** The Offers lens sees it as a real benefit workers would pay for, and as revenue. The Product lens sees it as charging workers to fix a problem the platform should remove, and wants prompt payment to be the default unless the CFO shows the fee is needed. The CFO's numbers decide this one.
- **Whether a moat is possible.** The Monopoly lens sees a real long-term moat. A pool of workers deep enough that organisers stop looking elsewhere, plus verified attendance records, would be hard to copy. But it scores the business lowest today, because that moat only exists at volume and leaks through direct hiring until then. The Offers and Product lenses care less about defence and more about whether the first booking is good enough to repeat.
- **What the ratings are worth.** The Product and Offers lenses see worker history as the answer to "will this person show up?". The Monopoly lens agrees, but only once there are enough of them. On day one nobody has a rating, so vetting new workers matters more than the rating system.

## The strongest version the board can see
The strongest version is **narrower than the pitch**: *reliable event crew for one kind of Lucknow event, guaranteed to show up*. Pick one buyer, such as wedding planners and caterers, or brands running promotions. Sell them an outcome rather than a list of people to browse: "Book 10 vetted staff for Saturday. If anyone doesn't turn up, we replace them or you don't pay." The organiser pays the commission. Workers join free, get paid reliably and quickly, and see their ratings unlock better-paid gigs. That paying-on-time promise and the no-show guarantee are what stop both sides going around the app. Start manually, on WhatsApp with a spreadsheet, for a few weeks of real bookings before building the app. Add the worker subscription only when there are more jobs than workers, and sell it as priority access, not "guaranteed events". This differs from the pitch, which leads with four event types, a worker subscription and the word "volunteer". The board thinks that version would struggle to fill either side.
