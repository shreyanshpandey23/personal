# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small, definable market.** One campus (BBD University) and one mall nearby is the kind of small starting market this lens likes. If the outlet is the obvious place for those students to eat, it can take a big share of a market that is small enough to win.
- **A clear position.** "100% pure veg, plant-powered" plus bowls, burritos, subs and cold brew under one roof is a sharp, easy-to-remember identity. Whether nobody else near campus holds that position is unconfirmed (/founder-competitors).
- **Brand work is done early.** A consistent brand is the one moat a single food outlet can realistically build, and the visual system is already there.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing stops a copycat.** Bowls, wraps, subs and cold brew are not proprietary. A nearby cafe or a chain already in the mall could add a "pure veg bowl" in month six. There is no technology, network effect or scale advantage, and a brand alone takes years to become a moat.
2. **Competing in a crowded, low-margin market.** A mall food court near a university likely already has several cheap veg options, and Swiggy and Zomato put this outlet next to every kitchen in delivery range. A ₹69 entry price suggests competing on price, which this lens sees as the path to thin margins. Who the rivals are and what they charge is an open question (/founder-competitors); whether ₹69 leaves any margin is an open question (/founder-cfo).
3. **No stated secret and no distribution plan.** The brief names no insight others are missing, and no plan for how the first 100 customers arrive beyond being in the mall and on delivery apps. Delivery-app commissions and discovery costs are unknown (/founder-cfo). Footfall and student numbers are unknown (/founder-consumer, /founder-competitors).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about BBD students' eating habits that the outlets already serving them do not?
- Who sells comparable food within walking or delivery distance of campus, and at what price?
- How do the first 100 customers find you, and what does each one cost to get?
- What stops the mall's existing outlets copying your menu within six months?
- Why now? What has changed that makes this the right moment?
- What is your food-service experience, and who on the team has run a kitchen? (asked about the business's execution risk, not as a judgement of you)
- After you own this campus, what is the next market?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A competitor map of every veg/quick-service option serving BBD students, in person and on delivery apps, that shows a real gap (/founder-competitors).
2. A named distribution engine for the campus (for example student groups, hostels or a loyalty/habit loop) with a cost per customer estimate (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo).
3. One thing that compounds and is hard to copy (a signature product, a supplier or recipe edge, or a campus-specific habit) chosen and written down before fit-out money is spent.
4. Unit economics showing the ₹69 items are not loss-making after delivery commissions (/founder-cfo).
