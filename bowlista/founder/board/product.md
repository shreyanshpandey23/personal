# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The brand work is far ahead of most first outlets.** It is one coherent system (cobalt and lime palette, rounded type, packaging for every format, uniforms, signage, social templates). That shows care for the details customers see.
- **The ₹69 mini tier is a clear first-five-minutes product.** It is a low-risk first try for a student, and a cheap way to sample the menu.
- **The "100% PURE VEG" promise is simple and easy to see.** It gives a clear reason to trust the kitchen, as long as it is kept everywhere.
- **The customer is tight and the place is fixed.** One campus and one mall make it possible to design the whole experience around a known daily routine.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No one thing.** Bowls, burritos, wraps, subs, sandwiches and cold brew are four formats, plus a coffee programme. The signage and the social posts don't even list them the same way ("Subs · Bowls · Wraps" vs "Bowls. Burritos. Subs."). Each format needs its own prep line, packaging and quality checks, so the craft gets spread thin and none of them becomes the reason to come.
2. **Delivery is a seam the business does not control.** On Swiggy and Zomato, the rider, the wait and the app listing all sit between the kitchen and the customer. A burrito or rice bowl that turns soggy or cold after travelling breaks the "Fresh" promise. Open question: does each item hold up after 20 to 30 minutes in its pack? Nothing in the brief tests this.
3. **The polish is ahead of the product.** Packaging and uniforms are finished, but there are no full-size prices, no food cost and no menu, and the brief does not say whether the Crown Mall site is secured. The brand currently promises an experience that has not been specified, built or tasted.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is Bowlista in one sentence, and which single item is it famous for?
- Walk me through a BBD student's first five minutes, at the counter and on the app. Where does it go wrong?
- What would you cut first if you could launch with only one format and one drink?
- How do you keep "100% pure veg" true through suppliers, shared equipment and delivery packaging?
- Is cold brew a reason to visit or just an add-on? Do students near campus want it? (/founder-consumer)
- Who else near campus already sells bowls, wraps or subs, and what do their reviews complain about? (/founder-competitors)
- Can a ₹69 mini make money, or is it a loss-leader? What does it cost to make? (/founder-cfo)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Name the hero.** Pick one hero format (likely the bowl, given the name) and cut or defer the rest. The menu, signage and posts should all say the same thing.
2. **Test delivery first.** Run a test showing that each item still meets the "Fresh" standard after a realistic delivery time, before it goes live on the apps.
3. **Write down the experience.** Describe the full experience from the counter to the app to the packaging to repeat orders, and name who owns each handover.
4. **Back the price with numbers.** Base full-menu pricing and the ₹69 tier on real food costs (/founder-cfo) and on tested student demand (/founder-consumer).
