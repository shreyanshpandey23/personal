# Board summary: Bowlista

## The vote
**3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass.** Average score **4.0 / 10** (Offers 4, Monopoly 3, Product 5).

No member wants to back it as pitched. All three would back a narrower version that has its numbers proven.

## Risks more than one member raised (most serious first)
1. **₹69 may lose money, and it invites a price war.** All three members raised this. Nobody knows yet whether a ₹69 mini covers food, packaging and the Swiggy/Zomato commission. Competing on price against existing campus and food-court options has no winning end. → `/founder-cfo`
2. **Nothing makes it the obvious choice, and nothing stops a copycat.** Offers and Monopoly both raised this. "Veg food from ₹69" is a commodity, and any outlet in the mall can add a veg bowl. The brand is good, but it isn't a moat yet. → `/founder-competitors`
3. **The menu is spread too thin.** Offers and Product both raised this. Bowls, burritos/wraps, subs and cold brew are four prep lines with no hero product, and the signage and the social posts don't even list them the same way.
4. **The brand is ahead of the business.** Product and Monopoly both raised this. Packaging and uniforms are finished, but there is no costed menu, no full-size prices, no confirmed site and no plan for winning the first 100 customers.

## Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)
- [ ] The ₹69 minis and a core bundle make money after food, packaging and aggregator commission, or the ₹69 item is deliberately priced as a loss-leader with a known cost (`/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] Full-menu prices are set from real food costs and tested student demand (`/founder-cfo`, `/founder-pricing`)
- [ ] A competitor map covers every veg and quick-service option serving BBD students, both in person and on delivery apps, and shows a real gap (`/founder-competitors`)
- [ ] The buyer panel shows demand above the price floor and names the exact buyer (hostel student, day scholar or late-night orderer) (`/founder-consumer`)
- [ ] One hero product is chosen (likely the bowl), and the other formats are cut or deferred, so the menu, signage and social posts all say the same thing (`/founder-offer`, `/founder-brand`)
- [ ] One named, stacked offer exists, for example a hostel meal pass or a weekly bowl + brew deal, with a guarantee the business can afford (`/founder-offer`)
- [ ] A distribution plan for campus exists (hostels, student groups, a loyalty/habit loop) with an estimated cost per customer (`/founder-marketing`)
- [ ] One hard-to-copy edge is written down before fit-out money is spent: a signature recipe, a supplier advantage or a campus habit (`/founder-offer`)
- [ ] A delivery test shows each item is still "Fresh" after 20–30 minutes in its pack (`/founder-launch`)
- [ ] The full experience is mapped from counter to app to packaging to repeat orders, with "100% pure veg" kept true through the supply chain (`/founder-ops`)
- [ ] Setup budget, Crown Mall rent and lease status are known (`/founder-cfo`, `/founder-ops`)

## Where the board disagrees
- **How strong the market is.** The Offers lens rates reach as the best thing about the idea: one campus, students who live on their phones. The Monopoly lens agrees the market is small and winnable, but argues that being close to students is not an advantage when every mall and Swiggy kitchen is just as close.
- **What the brand is worth.** The Product lens rates the finished brand system as a real strength and a sign of care for detail. The Monopoly lens says a brand is the only moat a single outlet can build, but it takes years, so it protects nothing today.
- **What the ₹69 tier is for.** The Product lens sees it as a smart first-try product. The Offers lens sees it as an invitation to a price war. Both can be true. The CFO's numbers will decide which.

## The strongest version the board can see
The strongest version is **narrower than the pitch**. It is *the bowl place for BBD students*. It has one hero: a filling, pure-veg rice bowl that travels well, built for hostel students tired of mess food. The ₹69 mini bowl is a deliberate first try, not the business. Money is made on a full bowl + cold brew combo and a weekly or monthly hostel meal pass that brings students back several times a week. That steady repeat business is the closest thing a single outlet has to a moat. Burritos and subs wait until the bowl is a habit. This differs from the pitch, which leads with four formats and a ₹69 headline price. The board thinks that version competes on price; this one competes on habit.
