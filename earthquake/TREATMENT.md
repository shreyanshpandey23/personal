# What Causes an Earthquake? — treatment

Style: Isometric Infographic (`iso-infographic`). ~31 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps. English narration (Kokoro, offline), subtitles as map-label cards, burned in + exported.

## Three candidate structures
1. **Top-to-bottom tilt through strata** — start on a calm town, dive down through soil and rock to the fault, then back up. Clear, but the cause (slow loading) gets one shot.
2. **Two boards compared** — "before" and "after" towns side by side. Shows the result, hides the mechanism.
3. **A cycle on one board, told with a crossing road** (chosen) — one diorama split by a fault. A road crosses it. The plates creep, the locked fault makes the road bend over "years" (Isotype year icons fill), the rock snaps, the road jumps straight again but offset, waves ring out from the focus to the town. This is elastic rebound, the real mechanism, and the road makes stress visible without a single equation.

Opening: **a question written as an isometric title on an empty board**. Ending: **scale reveal** — the camera pulls out until every label (plates, fault, focus, epicentre, waves) is on screen: the finished infographic, "NOT TO SCALE".

## Logline and arc
Two plates of crust grind past each other, but their edges stick; strain piles up for a century until the rock snaps and the stored energy shakes the town on top.
Setup (plates, fault) → loading (road bends, years pile up) → turn (snap, silence before it) → result (waves, shaking) → reveal (full infographic).

## Narration (Kokoro, `am_michael`-type calm male, short lines)
1. Why does the ground suddenly shake?
2. Earth's outer shell is broken into giant plates, always on the move.
3. Where two plates meet, their edges get stuck at a fault.
4. The plates keep pushing, and the rock bends, storing energy for years.
5. Until, suddenly, it snaps.
6. The energy races out as seismic waves, and the ground shakes.
7. That sudden slip is an earthquake.

## Cue map
96 bpm (beat 0.625 s). Kalimba motif on the title; marimba + shaker pulse during build; pizzicato ticks one per year icon, accelerating; low synth drone rising under the loading; 0.6 s digital silence before the snap; snap = crack + sub boom; rumble bed during waves; final pull-out: one kalimba note per label, resolve chord.
