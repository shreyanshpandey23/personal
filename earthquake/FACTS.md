# Facts in the film

Each claim is standard introductory seismology (elastic rebound theory, H. F. Reid, 1910). Sources to check against; wording below is a summary, not a quotation.

| Claim on screen / in narration | Source |
|---|---|
| Earth's outer shell (lithosphere) is broken into plates that move | USGS, "Understanding plate motions" — https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/understanding.html |
| Plates move a few centimetres a year | USGS, same page (rates of roughly 2–10 cm per year) |
| At a fault, friction locks the plate edges while the plates keep moving; the rock deforms elastically and stores strain energy | USGS, "The Science of Earthquakes" — https://earthquake.usgs.gov/learn/kids/eqscience.php |
| Strain builds over decades to centuries (shown as up to 150 years, an illustrative figure) | USGS, same page; recurrence intervals vary by fault |
| When stress exceeds the fault's strength the rock slips suddenly (elastic rebound); the released energy travels as seismic waves | USGS, same page |
| Focus (hypocentre) = where the slip starts underground; epicentre = the point on the surface directly above it | USGS, same page |

The diorama is NOT TO SCALE; the fault shown is a strike-slip fault (plates sliding past each other).
