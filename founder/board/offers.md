# Offers lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Buyers are concentrated and easy to reach.** One campus, one nearby mall, students who live on their phones. Reach is the strongest of the four market tests here.
- **Low entry price.** A ₹69 mini lowers the effort and risk of a first try. That helps the "likelihood" part of the value equation: the student risks little to find out.
- **The pure-veg badge is a real trust signal** in a market where some buyers will not risk a kitchen that also cooks meat. It is a believable promise, not just decoration.
- **Speed and low effort fit the format.** Grab-and-go bowls, wraps and subs, plus delivery, score well on time delay and effort.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **A commodity offer competing on price.** Today the offer is "veg food, starts at ₹69". Nothing in it makes a student feel stupid saying no, and any nearby rival can match a price. Student wallets are thin, so the business will be pushed into a price war it cannot win on margin. Whether ₹69 even covers food, packaging and an aggregator commission is unknown (/founder-cfo).
2. **The market fails the money and pain tests.** Hunger is frequent but not painful, and student spend per visit may be low. How many BBD students there are, Crown Mall footfall and typical spend are all open (/founder-consumer, /founder-competitors).
3. **Nothing stacked or guaranteed.** There are no bundles, no repeat-visit mechanic, no guarantee and no real urgency. A four-category menu with cold brew spreads the promise thin instead of making one outcome irresistible.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly is the buyer: the hostel student skipping mess food, the day scholar between classes, or the late-night delivery orderer? What do they eat now, and at what price? (/founder-competitors)
- What does a student get here that they cannot get at the nearest campus canteen or food court?
- After food cost, packaging and aggregator commission, what does a ₹69 mini actually earn? (/founder-cfo)
- What would a student pay for a full bowl plus a cold brew, and would they come back every week? (/founder-consumer)
- What is the guarantee, for example on delivery time, freshness or "fill you up"?
- Could you charge more and still be the obvious choice? If not, why not?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Build one named, stacked offer for one buyer (for example, a meal pass or a weekly bowl-plus-brew plan for hostel students), with a guarantee the CFO says the business can afford.
2. Unit economics showing the ₹69 entry item and the core bundle make money after commission (/founder-cfo).
3. Buyer-panel evidence of demand and a price above the floor, plus a competitor map showing the gap is real (/founder-consumer, /founder-competitors).
